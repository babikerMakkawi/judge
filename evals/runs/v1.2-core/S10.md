Don't commit the $20k to anything yet. For the next six months its job is to pay your bills, not to grow. Keep it in cash or a high-yield savings account until you know what the time is for.

Three things hold whatever you choose:

1. Add up your monthly spending, including rent, food, bills, debt payments and health cover. Divide $20,000 by that number. At about $3,333 a month the money lasts exactly six months with nothing left. To also keep a two-month buffer in case the job start slips, spending has to stay near $2,500 a month.
2. Check whether the start date is firm (a signed offer with a date) or only expected. If it is firm, six months is a real plan. If not, plan for longer.
3. Decide now on a spending cap for anything extra you want to do with the time, such as courses, travel or a side project, so it cannot eat the buffer.

Risks to check:

- Runway: the most likely failure is a start date that moves. The buffer in point 1 is what protects you.
- Health cover and tax: if your current coverage ends with the job, you need replacement cover from day one. Any freelance or side income can create tax bills you have not budgeted for.
- Debt: if you carry credit card or other high-interest debt, paying it down will likely beat most safe returns. Keep a cash cushion while you do it.
- Investing: six months is too short for stocks. A market drop could cut the money just when you need it, so keep it in cash.
- Lock-up: avoid anything you cannot withdraw quickly, such as fixed-term deposits or a business with upfront costs.
