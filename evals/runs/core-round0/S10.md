## baseline / haiku
### t1
Six months with $20k is a good position, and the main decision is how much of that $20k to keep as a cushion.

**1. Set aside your safety net first.** Before spending on anything, figure out your monthly essentials (rent, food, insurance, debt minimums, phone, transport). Keep at least 3 months of that in a high-yield savings account so you're not forced into a rushed job offer if the search runs long. If your new job is likely to pay less, or you may want to relocate, keep more.

**2. Check the things that cost money if you're between jobs.**
- **Health insurance:** If you're losing employer coverage, you usually get a special enrollment window (about 60 days) for a Marketplace plan or COBRA. Price both now, and find out whether your new employer's coverage starts right away or after a waiting period.
- **Unemployment benefits:** If you were laid off, you may qualify. If you quit, you usually don't. Apply right away, since benefits often start from the date you file.
- **Taxes:** If you have a sizable balance in a tax-advantaged account, or you'll have no income for part of the year, check whether you need to make estimated payments or can owe less than you think.
- **Debt:** If you carry high-interest debt, paying it down is a guaranteed return. Pay off anything above roughly 7-8% before investing.

**3. Use the time for things that pay off later.**
- **Job search, done deliberately:** Update your résumé, tell your network you're looking, and apply to a short list of roles you actually want. Money isn't the constraint here; your time is, so don't let it drift.
- **Skills:** If your field is changing, take one practical course or build one project that you can show an interviewer. Pick something with a clear output, not a long, vague program.
- **Health and routine:** A steady schedule helps the search. Exercise, sleep and time away from screens matter more when you have no structure.
- **Rest, within reason:** A week or two off after a layoff is reasonable and can make you more effective later. Six months of unstructured time can slide into drift, so set weekly goals.

**4. Put the rest to work, for the long term.**
- If you have no high-interest debt and your emergency fund is covered, invest the leftover money in a diversified, low-cost approach, such as a broad index fund, for money you won't need for at least 5 years.
- Keep money you might need within 1-2 years in cash or a high-yield savings account or Treasury bills. Don't put it in stocks.
- Don't use money you'll need for living expenses to chase high returns. Market drops can last longer than your runway.

**A rough split** if you have no debt and a stable plan: about $10k-12k kept liquid for living costs (as your emergency fund), about $2k-3k for insurance, moving, and a course or two, and the rest invested if you won't need it for several years.
