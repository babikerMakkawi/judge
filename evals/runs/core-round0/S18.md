**Why not A?** It's the best one to carry (1.3 kg, 15h battery), but it's weaker where you need power:
- **ARM chip:** VMs only run at full speed if the guest OS is also ARM, such as ARM Linux or Windows on ARM. Any x86 VM has to be emulated, which is very slow. That includes a ready-made VM image a course gives you. Most popular Docker images now come in ARM versions, but x86-only ones also need emulation.
- **16GB of RAM:** That gets tight once a VM, containers, an IDE and a browser are all running at once.

If you're sure you'll only ever need ARM-compatible VMs and images, A is a strong choice. Otherwise, x86 is the safer bet for a CS degree.

**Why not B?** 32GB and 1TB for $1,099 is excellent value, but 2.1 kg is heavy to carry all day. Its 6h rated battery will also run out well before the end of a campus day, and faster with VMs running. It's better as a desk machine.

**Before you buy:**
- C is only $49 over budget. A 4% student discount or a sale would bring it under $1,300. If you find that, get C instead: it has 32GB of RAM, it's lighter and its battery lasts longer.
- If your $1,300 also has to cover sales tax, E goes over budget once tax is above about 4%. A student discount would help there too.
