I can't give you a business plan for this version, because it can't run: solar panels produce nothing between 10pm and 4am, and with no batteries and no grid there is no other source. The club would be dark and silent during every hour it is open. Investors being excited doesn't change that, and it's better to find out now than after the roof is built.

The goal underneath it (a genuinely low-carbon club) is workable. These are the realistic versions:

| Option | How it works | Fits "eco"? | Main catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Solar + batteries, no grid | Panels charge a battery bank by day; the bank runs the night | Strongest claim | Battery cost and a roof big enough for the load; winter and cloudy weeks are the weak point |
| B. Solar + batteries + grid/generator backup | Same as A, with a fallback | Strong, and honest if you report the share that is solar | Slightly weaker marketing line, much lower risk of a dead night |
| C. Grid on a renewable tariff, solar offsets the daytime | Roof feeds the grid; you buy green power at night | Good, but the "100% solar" claim is not accurate | Claim must be worded carefully or it is greenwashing |
| D. Daytime or sunset club | Open roughly 2pm to 10pm so the panels power the event live | Literal 100% direct solar | Different product, different crowd, and licensing and noise rules differ by city |
| E. Your original plan | Solar only, 10pm to 4am | No | Zero power at opening time |

My pick is B, with A as the ambitious version if the numbers allow. D is the only way to keep "no batteries, no grid", but it is a different business.

Sizing for A or B, with every number an assumption you must replace with your own:
- Suppose the venue averages 25 kW over the six open hours (sound, lighting, cooling, bars, refrigeration). That is about 150 kWh per night.
- The battery has to cover that plus losses and a reserve, so plan for something noticeably larger than 150 kWh, and panels large enough to refill it daily.
- Check whether your roof area can supply that. Many club roofs cannot, and if yours can't, the plan becomes B.
- Get your real load from an electrician or sound/lighting supplier before anything else. Dance-floor sound and cooling for a packed room are the big draws.

What the plan must prove before investors commit:
1. Energy: a load audit and a quote from a solar installer who has done battery-backed commercial systems. This is the first gate.
2. Demand: nobody has shown me that your area wants an eco club or what they would pay. Test with a few pop-up nights on rented power before building anything.
3. Cash: battery and panel capex, fit-out, licensing and a runway of several months of losses. Prepaid items don't count as runway.
4. Break-even: write a low, expected and high table for covers per night, average spend, and nights per week (clubs are often busy only on Fridays and Saturdays).
5. Licences and noise: late-night alcohol and noise permits are the usual killer. Check the venue, hours and zoning before signing a lease.
6. Claims: if you market "100% solar", it must be true on the night. Otherwise say "X% solar" and keep the records.
7. Failure case: decide what happens if the batteries run low at 2am (a backup, a shortened night, a refund policy).
8. Exit and lock-in: lease length, equipment financing, and what the battery is worth if the club closes.

Cheapest test: run two or three one-off events using rented batteries or a generator and sell tickets. If people show up and pay, you have real demand data and a measured power load, which makes everything else in the plan believable.

Stop condition: if the installer's quote plus fit-out can't be covered by realistic ticket and bar revenue in the low case, don't build it as a permanent venue. Do the pop-up version or option D instead.
