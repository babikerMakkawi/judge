**No, don't quit for this idea.** The problem isn't the $5 price or the subscriber count. As described, every subscriber loses you money, so 2,000 of them would make the loss bigger.

The decisive reason is arithmetic. $5 a month is about $1.15 a week per subscriber (assuming 4.33 weeks a month). That has to cover the ice, an insulated box and postage anywhere in the US. Postage for a heavy parcel alone is very likely above $1.15, but get a real quote to confirm.

The second problem is that ice melts. After 3 to 5 days in the mail, most of each bag will likely arrive as meltwater. Also check with USPS whether they accept frozen goods at all. Don't assume they do.

Here is the per-subscriber math. The cost figures are placeholder assumptions, not quotes. Replace them with real numbers.

| | Low | Expected | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost per weekly bag (ice, box, postage) | $6 | $12 | $20 |
| Revenue per weekly bag | $1.15 | $1.15 | $1.15 |
| Loss per subscriber per week | -$4.85 | -$10.85 | -$18.85 |
| Monthly loss at 2,000 subscribers | about -$42k | about -$94k | about -$163k |

Revenue at 2,000 subscribers is about $10,000 a month in every case. Adding subscribers can't reach break-even. Break-even needs a delivered cost under $1.15 per bag, including postage, and that isn't realistic for shipped ice.

**Risks, ranked by severity**
1. **Unit economics (fatal):** postage alone may exceed what a subscriber pays. Check: get a quote for a 1 lb, 5 lb and 10 lb package to a distant zone.
2. **Product survival (fatal):** the ice may not arrive as ice. Check: ship one insulated test package to a far address and weigh what is left on arrival.
3. **Demand:** nothing shows people want weekly ice by mail rather than buying it locally. Check: run a waitlist for a local version and count real sign-ups. Don't take payments yet.
4. **Personal runway:** quitting means funding your living costs while the business loses money. Keep your income until items 1 and 2 pass.

**What might be real:** people do buy ice for parties, coolers, camping and events, and that demand is local and urgent. A same-day or next-day local ice delivery service, priced per order or per event, avoids the shipping problem entirely. First check whether ice suppliers near you already offer this.
